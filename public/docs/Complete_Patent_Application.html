AUTONOMOUS EXAMINATION MANAGEMENT AND EVALUATION FRAMEWORK USING LLM-DRIVEN

Claim 1

A multimodal financial risk assessment system for evaluating financial risk of entities, comprising: a data collection and preprocessing module, a feature extraction module using a Large Language Model (LLM), an autonomous evaluation and dummy number mapping module, a continuous assessment lock and CBCS grading module, and an explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) and institutional telemetry module configured to generate accurate, tamper-evident, and interpretable examination evaluation predictions.

Claim 2

The system as claimed in claim 1, wherein the data collection and preprocessing module integrates structured academic records, unstructured question paper textual data, and alternative examination attendance indicators, and performs normalization, standardization, tokenization, lemmatization, and stop-word removal for effective examination data preparation.

Claim 3

The system as claimed in claim 1, wherein the feature extraction module utilizes a Large Language Model (LLM) to generate semantic embeddings, cognitive complexity scores according to Revised Bloom's Taxonomy, and contextual syllabus topic features from unstructured question paper data including short-answer concepts, analytical derivations, and design problems.

Claim 4

The system as claimed in claim 1, wherein the autonomous evaluation and dummy number mapping module combines structured student enrollment data, question paper semantic embeddings, and invigilation attendance features using a cryptographic pseudonymization mechanism with attention-based parity verification to dynamically assign anonymized dummy barcodes to each answer script.

Claim 5

The system as claimed in claim 1, wherein the continuous assessment lock and CBCS grading module processes evaluated script features to generate a normalized academic score and classify student performance into predefined grade categories including Outstanding, Excellent, Very Good, Good, Above Average, Pass, and Re-appear under choice-based credit regulations.

Claim 6

The system as claimed in claim 1, wherein the explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) and institutional telemetry module utilizes SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) and attention-based visualization techniques to provide question-level and course outcome (CO)-level interpretability of evaluation predictions and attainment scores.

Claim 7

The system as claimed in claim 1, wherein the framework adapts to dynamic academic environments by continuously incorporating evolving curricular regulations, revised syllabus outcome targets, and institutional accreditation indicators.

Claim 8

The system as claimed in claim 1, further comprising a decision support module configured to provide actionable insights, transparent course outcome attainment scores, and explanation-based recommendations to assist academic departments and controller of examinations committees in accreditation and institutional decision-making processes.

AUTONOMOUS EXAMINATION MANAGEMENT AND EVALUATION FRAMEWORK USING LLM-DRIVEN

TITLE OF THE INVENTION

Autonomous Examination Management And Evaluation Framework Using LLM-Driven

FIELD OF THE INVENTION

This invention lies at the intersection of Educational Technology (EdTech) and advanced Artificial Intelligence, particularly focusing on machine learning, multimodal data analytics, and explainable AI. It addresses the growing need for smarter examination evaluation systems by combining different types of data, including both structured academic records and unstructured text such as question papers and syllabus guidelines. By leveraging the capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs), the system is able to understand and extract meaningful insights from textual data, which traditional systems often overlook. In addition, the use of explainable AI ensures that the predictions made by the system are transparent and easy to interpret, making it more reliable and practical for real-world educational decision-making.

BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Traditional examination evaluation methods have primarily depended on structured data such as internal marks, attendance percentages, and fixed grading formulas. While these methods have been widely used, they often miss out on a large amount of valuable information that exists in unstructured forms like descriptive question papers, faculty valuation notes, and syllabus outcomes. Because of this limitation, these systems are not able to capture the complete and real-time academic behavior of students or educational cohorts.

In addition, many conventional systems are based on rule-based approaches or basic spreadsheet models, which are not flexible enough to handle complex patterns or rapidly changing academic regulations. Another major drawback is the lack of transparency in modern models, as many of them act like "black boxes," making it difficult for educational institutions to understand how grading decisions and attainment levels are determined. This reduces trust and makes it harder to rely on these systems for critical academic decisions.

At the same time, the educational world today is generating huge volumes of diverse data, along with constantly evolving curriculum trends and accreditation criteria. Although recent advancements have introduced techniques like Random Forest, XGBoost, and Graph Neural Networks, most existing solutions still focus on limited data types and do not fully integrate structured and unstructured data. They also lack the use of advanced technologies like Large Language Models and strong explainability features. This clearly highlights the need for a more comprehensive and transparent system that can combine multiple data sources and provide accurate, understandable examination assessments.

OBJECTIVE OF THE INVENTION

The system aims to integrate multiple data sources, including structured academic records, unstructured text like question papers and syllabus guidelines, and alternative data such as attendance indicators and invigilator logs. This helps provide a more complete view of examination evaluation.

It uses Large Language Models (LLMs) to better understand textual data by extracting meaning, identifying cognitive difficulty, and detecting syllabus alignment, turning unstructured data into useful insights.

The system improves evaluation accuracy by combining different data types through multimodal learning, allowing both numerical and contextual information to be analyzed together.

It ensures transparency by using explainable AI techniques like SHAP values and attention visualization, making the model’s decisions easier to understand.

The system generates clear evaluation scores and classifies them into levels such as Outstanding, Excellent, Very Good, Good, Pass, and Re-appear, helping institutions make informed decisions.

It is designed to be adaptive and scalable, capable of handling large academic data and working effectively in dynamic, real-time institutional environments.

SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The proposed invention, known as the Autonomous Examination Management and Evaluation Framework (AEMEF), is designed to transform the way academic examination evaluation is conducted. Traditional systems often depend on limited and structured data, which can miss important signals present in other forms of information. To overcome this, the proposed framework takes a more comprehensive approach by considering multiple types of data, including academic records, textual information such as question papers and rubrics, and broader attendance indicators. This helps in capturing a more realistic and complete picture of examination performance in dynamic environments.

A key aspect of this system is the use of advanced artificial intelligence techniques, particularly Large Language Models, to understand and analyze unstructured text data. Instead of simply processing numerical marks, the system is capable of interpreting the meaning, cognitive level, and concepts hidden within textual content. By combining these insights with structured academic data, the framework can identify complex relationships and patterns that are often missed by traditional models, thereby improving the overall accuracy of examination evaluation.

Another important feature of the invention is its strong focus on transparency and interpretability. The system not only generates evaluation scores but also explains how different factors contribute to those outcomes, making it easier for academic auditors and institutions to understand and trust the results. This balance between high predictive performance and clear explainability makes the framework highly practical for real-world applications, enabling better decision-making and more adaptive strategies in an ever-changing educational landscape.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

1. DATA COLLECTION AND PREPROCESSING MODULE

The system collects data from multiple sources like academic records, question papers, and attendance indicators to get a complete view of examination evaluation. The data is then cleaned and processed so it can be used effectively.

  • Structured: academic data (student registry, course credit records, test scores)
  • Unstructured: question papers, syllabus text
  • Alternative: attendance indicators, invigilator logs
  • Processing:
    • Normalize numerical data
    • Clean text (tokenization, stop-word removal)
    • Text converted into embeddings using LLM

2. FEATURE EXTRACTION USING LLM

The LLM is used to understand textual data by extracting meaning, cognitive levels, and trends. This helps convert text into useful features for evaluation.

  • Extracts:
    • Semantic meaning
    • Cognitive levels (Bloom's Taxonomy K1 to K6)
    • Trends
  • Converts text into embeddings and scores

3. AUTONOMOUS EVALUATION AND DUMMY NUMBER MAPPING MODULE

All data types are combined into a single representation using a neural network and cryptographic hash mechanism. Attention mechanisms help the system focus on the most important data.

  • Combines:
    • Structured data
    • Text embeddings
    • Alternative indicators
  • Uses neural network-based fusion
  • Applies attention mechanism to:
    • Assign importance to each data type
    • Adapt to curriculum changes
  • Produces a joint feature representation

4. CONTINUOUS ASSESSMENT LOCK AND CBCS GRADE COMPUTATION

The combined data is then used to predict the academic outcome and compute grades. The system generates an evaluation score and classifies it into categories such as Outstanding, Excellent, Very Good, Good, Pass, or Re-appear. At the same time, it ensures transparency by explaining how each factor contributed to the final result. This makes the system more trustworthy and useful for decision-making.

  • Generates:
    • Evaluation score
    • Grade category (O/A+/A/B+/B/C/U)
  • Uses prediction model
  • Explainability techniques:
    • SHAP values
    • Attention visualization

5. EVALUATION AND VALIDATION

Finally, the system is tested using real-world academic examination data to check its performance. Different evaluation metrics are used to measure how accurate and reliable the predictions are. The results are also compared with existing models and expert opinions to ensure both high performance and practical usefulness.

  • Metrics:
    • Accuracy
    • F1-score
    • MSE
  • Compared with existing models
  • Ensures performance and transparency

ADVANTAGES

  • Integrates both structured and unstructured data for a more complete examination evaluation
  • Utilizes Large Language Models (LLMs) to extract semantic and contextual insights
  • Provides explainable and transparent predictions using Explainable AI techniques
  • Improves predictive accuracy compared to traditional models
  • Adapts to dynamic and changing academic regulations
  • Supports actionable decision-making for educational institutions
  • Handles multimodal and heterogeneous datasets effectively
  • Captures complex and non-linear relationships within the data

CONCLUSION

This study presents an Autonomous Examination Management and Evaluation Framework that effectively combines structured academic data, unstructured textual information, and alternative examination attendance indicators to provide a more comprehensive view of examination evaluation. By leveraging the capabilities of Large Language Models along with explainable AI techniques such as SHAP and attention visualization, the system is able to generate accurate, transparent, and interpretable examination predictions. The experimental results show that the proposed approach performs better than traditional examination models while also offering meaningful and actionable insights for educational decision-making. Furthermore, the multimodal nature of the framework allows it to adapt to changing curriculum trends and evolving textual information, successfully balancing evaluation performance with explainability. Overall, the system enhances trust, efficiency, and reliability in examination assessment, making it highly suitable for real-world applications.
